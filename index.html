01 — OVERVIEW

What is Threshold ML-DSA?

ML-DSA — formerly Dilithium — is one of NIST's standardized post-quantum digital signature algorithms. SHIELD extends it with threshold cryptography: a single private key never exists in one place.

Instead, key shares are distributed across multiple parties who must cooperate to produce a signature. The result is quantum-resistant security and distributed trust — no single party can sign alone, and no single point of compromise can forge.

Built on EMP-toolkit with hybrid SPDZ + WRK17 protocols and Bristol-format circuits, SHIELD is a research-grade reference for federal PKI and enterprise certificate authorities preparing for the post-quantum transition.

  • [01]

    Distributed key generation

    Private keys are split across parties from the moment of creation. No single machine ever holds the full secret.

  • [02]

    Cooperative signing

    Signatures require a threshold of parties to participate. Compromise of any single share leaks nothing.

  • [03]

    Quantum-resistant

    Built on lattice-based ML-DSA, secure against both classical and quantum cryptanalysis.

  • [04]

    Federal PKI ready

    FIPS-204 compliant, designed for government and enterprise certificate authority deployment.

03 — ARCHITECTURE

Hybrid MPC, built to spec.

SHIELD layers a distributed signing protocol on top of the FIPS-204 ML-DSA primitive. Modular arithmetic over the prime q = 2²³ − 2¹³ + 1 is handled by the SPDZ protocol; bit-level operations such as rejection sampling and hashing are evaluated as WRK17 Boolean circuits.

All threshold-specific code lives under src/include/ml_dsa/threshold/, prefixed threshold_*. The standard verification function is unmodified — verifiers don't need to know a signature was produced by a quorum.

04 — APPLICATIONS

Where threshold trust matters most.

▸ GOVERNMENT

Federal Public Key Infrastructure

Distribute root and intermediate signing authority across agencies and HSMs. No single compromise — internal or external — can mint rogue certificates.

▸ ENTERPRISE

Certificate Authorities

Replace single-key CAs with quorum-based signing. Audit, rotate, and recover without ever reconstructing a full secret key.

▸ INFRASTRUCTURE

Code & Firmware Signing

Require cooperation from security, release, and compliance teams before any production binary is signed. Quantum-safe out of the box.

▸ RESEARCH

Distributed Trust Systems

A reference implementation for academic exploration of threshold lattice-based signatures, MPC optimization, and protocol analysis.
